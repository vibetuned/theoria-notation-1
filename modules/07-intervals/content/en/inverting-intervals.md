# Inversion: turning an interval over

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. XVII sec. 195, p. 86. Original prose.

An interval is **inverted** when its upper tone becomes the lower one —
move the lower tone up an octave and you have it. Gehrkens' example:
C–E, a major third, becomes E–C, a minor sixth. Two habits make
inversions easy to name: the two numbers always add up to nine (3 + 6,
5 + 4), and the qualities trade places — major with minor, augmented
with diminished — while perfect stays perfect.
