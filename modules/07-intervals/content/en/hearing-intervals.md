# Hearing intervals

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. XVII pp. 82–86. Original prose; the figures are the book's
own (`assets/book/fig62-63.musicxml`, `fig64.musicxml`).

Music has four elements — rhythm, melody, harmony, and tone color — and
the **interval** is where melody and harmony meet: two tones sounded
**together** make a *harmonic* interval, **one after another** a
*melodic* one. Same distance, two guises.

Naming is a two-step method. First the **number**: count the staff
degrees the two notes span — C up to G♯ is still a *fifth*, whatever the
accidental. Then the **quality**: count half steps. Measured up from the
tonic, every interval of the major scale is **major or perfect** (fig.
64); each grows or shrinks a semitone at a time through the size ladder
*diminished < minor < major < augmented*, and inverted intervals always
sum to **nine**.

## Narration (video: HearingIntervals)

1. Two tones together: a harmonic interval. The same two in succession:
   melodic. One distance, two ways of wearing it.
2. Above the tonic, the scale hands you the reference set — everything
   major or perfect.
3. Naming takes two counts: staff degrees give the number; half steps
   give the quality. C to G-sharp is a fifth before it is anything else.
4. And the ladder orders the rest: diminished, minor, major, augmented —
   with inversions always summing to nine.
