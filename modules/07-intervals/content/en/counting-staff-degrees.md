# The number of an interval: count the staff degrees

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. XVII sec. 184 (1), pp. 83–84. Original prose.

The first half of an interval's name is its **number**: second, third,
fifth and so on. It comes from counting staff degrees — lines and
spaces — from one tone to the other, both ends included: C up to E
covers C, D, E, so it is a third. Sharps and flats never enter the
count. Gehrkens' own examples make the point: C–G♯, C♯–G♭ and C♭–G♯ are
all fifths, even though they sound quite different from C–G.
