# The quality of an interval: count the half steps

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. XVII secs. 184 (2), 186–187, pp. 83–85. Original prose.

The second half of an interval's name — major, minor, perfect,
diminished, augmented — is its **quality**, and the ruler for it is the
half step. Three seconds: B–C spans one half step (minor), B–C♯ two
(major), F–G♯ three (augmented). Three thirds: C–E double-flat spans
two half steps (diminished), C–E♭ three (minor), C–E four (major). The
staff degrees gave the number; the half steps settle the quality.

- unit: The number is only half the name. The quality is measured in half steps.
- minor-second: B up to C: one half step, a minor 2nd.
- major-second: B up to C♯: two half steps, a major 2nd.
- augmented-second: F up to G♯: three half steps, an augmented 2nd.
- label-m2: minor 2nd
- label-M2: major 2nd
- label-A2: augmented 2nd
- seconds: Three seconds, three sizes: the half steps decide the quality.
- diminished-third: C up to E double-flat: two half steps, a diminished 3rd.
- minor-third: C up to E♭: three half steps, a minor 3rd.
- major-third: C up to E: four half steps, a major 3rd.
- label-d3: diminished 3rd
- label-m3: minor 3rd
- label-M3: major 3rd
- thirds: Same number, different sizes: 2, 3 or 4 half steps.
- remember: The number comes from the staff degrees; the quality from the half steps.
