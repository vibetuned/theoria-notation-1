# The seven triads of C major

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. XVIII sec. 198, pp. 87–88 (fig. 65). Original prose.

A triad can stand on any degree of the scale. Build one on each degree
of C major, using only the scale's own tones, and three kinds turn up:
major on 1, 4 and 5; minor on 2, 3 and 6; diminished on 7. A Roman
numeral names the degree, and — as Gehrkens explains — its size names
the kind: a large numeral for major, a small one for minor, a small one
with a circle for diminished. The triads on I, IV and V are used so much
more than the rest that they are called the **common chords**.
