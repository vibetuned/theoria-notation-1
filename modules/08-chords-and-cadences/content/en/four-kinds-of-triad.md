# Four kinds of triad

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. XVIII secs. 196–197, p. 87. Original prose.

A chord is several tones sounding together, and the simplest chord is
the **triad**: a root with the third and the fifth above it. Triads come
in four kinds, and two thirds stacked on the root tell them apart. A
major third with a minor third on top is a **major** triad; the reverse,
a **minor** triad; two minor thirds make a **diminished** triad, two
major thirds an **augmented** one. Gehrkens also measures from the root
to the top: major and minor triads span a perfect fifth, the diminished
triad a diminished fifth, the augmented triad an augmented fifth.
