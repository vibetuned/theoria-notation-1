# Cadences in G and F: find the missing bass

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. XVIII secs. 203–207, pp. 89–91. Original prose; the chords
are the cadence worksheets' voicings, moved to G and F major.

The cadence worksheets write each cadence out in C, G or F major with
one bass note missing. The rule that finds it never changes: the bass
of each chord is its **root**, the scale degree the numeral names. In G
major, V stands on D and I on G; vi on E. In F major, IV stands on B♭,
V on C and I on F. Count up the scale to the numeral's degree and you
have the note to play.

## On screen (video: CadencesInGAndF)

- title: Cadences in G and F: find the bass
- worksheet: The worksheets move the cadences to G and F major, with one bass note missing.
- example-1: G major | authentic cadence | V → I | D → ? | D → G
- ask-1: The bass of I is missing. In G major, which tone is I built on?
- answer-1: G, the 1st degree. The bass is the chord's root.
- example-2: G major | deceptive cadence | V → vi | D → ? | D → E
- ask-2: Now vi has no bass. Count up G major to the 6th degree.
- answer-2: E, the root of vi. V to vi: deceptive.
- example-3: F major | plagal cadence | IV → I | ? → F | B♭ → F
- ask-3: In F major, one flat, IV has lost its bass. What is the 4th degree?
- answer-3: B♭, the root of IV. B♭ down to F: plagal.
- example-4: F major | half cadence | I → V | F → ? | F → C
- ask-4: A half cadence in F: which root does V need?
- answer-4: C, the 5th degree. The phrase stops on V.
- remember: The missing bass is the chord's root: count up the scale to the numeral's degree.
