# The seven triads of A minor

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. XVIII sec. 198, p. 88 (the minor-key triads figure).
Original prose.

Gehrkens builds the triads of a minor key on its **harmonic** form, so
in A minor every chord that contains a G takes G♯. The result is a
richer family than in major: i and iv are minor, V and VI major, ii° and
vii° diminished — and III+ is augmented, two major thirds stacked. The
raised 7th is the reason for it all: it turns V into a major chord whose
G♯ leads straight home to the tonic.
