# Why "minor"? The smaller third

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. VIII secs. 84–85 and 89, pp. 33–35. Original prose.

Major and minor take their names from a single interval: the third,
measured from the 1st tone of the scale up to the 3rd. In major it
spans four half steps; in minor only three — the *smaller* third.
Build the scale on that smaller third and you have the minor scale in
its oldest, **primitive** form: whole, half, whole, whole, half, whole,
whole. A minor uses exactly the tones of C major, and so the same
signature; keys that share a signature are **relative** keys.

- names: Major and minor are named after one interval: the third, from 1 up to 3.
- major-third: A up to C♯: four half steps. The larger third: major.
- minor-third: A up to C: three half steps. The smaller third: minor.
- primitive: With the smaller third: A minor, primitive form. W H W W H W W.
- relative: Same tones, same signature as C major: A minor is its relative minor.
- remember: Minor means a smaller third: 3 half steps, not 4. Relative keys share one signature.
