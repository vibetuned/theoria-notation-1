# All the scales

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), chs. VII–VIII pp. 27–41. Original prose; the scales are built
live, every key shown twice — once as the signature says it, once
spelled out.

The major scale is one pattern — half steps between 3–4 and 7–8 — living
at **fifteen addresses**. Each address can be written two ways: the
**key signature** states the pattern's accidentals once at the clef, or
the scale spells them **note by note**. Seeing the same ladder both ways
is what makes signatures stop being arbitrary: the signature *is* the
scale, written once.

Minor triples the tour. Each of the fifteen minor keys (sharing its
relative major's signature) wears three shapes: the **primitive**
(natural) form — the signature alone; the **harmonic** — the seventh
raised into a leading tone, leaving the step-and-a-half from 6 to 7; and
the **melodic** — 6 and 7 both raised on the ascent. Whatever rises,
rises as an **accidental**: the signature never changes.

## Narration (video: AllTheMajorScales)

1. Every major key, two ways: above, the signature at the clef; below,
   the same scale with every accidental written where it sounds.
2. C major first — nothing to write. Then the sharps arrive one by one,
   G through C-sharp; then the flats, F through C-flat.
3. Listen and watch each one: the pattern never changes, only the
   address. The signature is the scale, written once.

## Narration (video: AllTheMinorScales)

1. Every minor key, three shapes — first the primitive form, exactly what
   the signature says.
2. Then the harmonic: the seventh raised by accidental, and the
   unmistakable wide step it leaves behind.
3. Then the melodic ascent: six and seven both raised, smoothing the
   climb home.
4. Fifteen keys, three forms, every note sounded. The signature belongs
   to the primitive form — everything raised is spelled by hand.
