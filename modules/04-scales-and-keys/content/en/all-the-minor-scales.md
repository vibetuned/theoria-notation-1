# All the minor scales

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. VIII pp. 33–41. Original prose; the scales are built live,
each key's three forms drawn and sounded in turn.

Minor triples the tour. Each of the fifteen minor keys — sharing its
relative major's signature — wears three shapes: the **primitive**
(natural) form, which is the signature alone; the **harmonic**, with
the seventh raised into a leading tone, leaving the step-and-a-half
between 6 and 7; and the **melodic**, with 6 and 7 both raised on the
ascent and restored on the way down. One promise never breaks: whatever
rises, rises as an **accidental** — the signature belongs to the
primitive form and never changes.

## Narration (video: AllTheMinorScales)

1. Every minor key, three shapes — first the primitive form, exactly what
   the signature says.
2. Then the harmonic: the seventh raised by accidental, and the
   unmistakable wide step it leaves behind.
3. Then the melodic ascent: six and seven both raised, smoothing the
   climb home.
4. Fifteen keys, three forms, every note sounded. The signature belongs
   to the primitive form — everything raised is spelled by hand.
