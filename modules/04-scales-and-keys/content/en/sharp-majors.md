# The sharp major keys, written two ways

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. VII sec. 83 and its note, pp. 30–32. Original prose.

Gehrkens' advice for learning the keys is to write every scale twice
over: first with each sharp or flat placed before its own note, then
with the same accidentals gathered at the clef as the signature. This
tour does exactly that for C major and the seven sharp keys, G through
C♯. Each key adds one sharp, and each sharp in the spelled-out scale
lights up its twin in the signature — the signature is nothing but the
scale's accidentals, written once.

## On screen (video: SharpMajors)

- title: The sharp keys, written two ways
- keys: C major, G major, D major, A major, E major, B major, F♯ major, C♯ major
- counts: no sharps or flats, 1 sharp, 2 sharps, 3 sharps, 4 sharps, 5 sharps, 6 sharps, 7 sharps
- two-ways: Above: the key signature. Below: the scale, with each accidental by its note.
- c-major: C major first: nothing to write, in either place.
- one-more: Each new key adds one sharp. Each one lights up in both places.
- twins: Every sharp in the scale has its twin in the signature.
- last: F♯ and C♯ major: six and seven sharps.
- written-once: The signature is the scale's sharps, written once at the clef.
- remember: A key signature is its scale's accidentals, written once at the clef.
