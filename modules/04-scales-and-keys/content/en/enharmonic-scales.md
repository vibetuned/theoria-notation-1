# Same tones, two spellings: enharmonic scales

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. VII sec. 83, p. 32. Original prose.

Fifteen ways to write the major scale, but only twelve different
starting keys on the piano: three pairs of scales use exactly the same
keys while looking completely different on paper. F♯ major (six
sharps) and G♭ major (six flats) sound identical; so do B and C♭, and
C♯ and D♭. Scales like these, alike in sound but not in notation, are
called **enharmonic**.
