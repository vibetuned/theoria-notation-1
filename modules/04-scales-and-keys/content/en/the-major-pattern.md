# The major scale: one pattern

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. VII sec. 81, p. 29. Original prose.

A major scale climbs eight tones, and the distances between neighbours
follow one fixed plan: whole steps everywhere, except two half steps —
from the 3rd tone to the 4th, and from the 7th to the 8th. On C the plan
needs no black keys at all. Start it anywhere else and the plan decides
which tones must be sharpened or flattened: from D, F and C become F♯
and C♯. That is why Gehrkens insists there is really only one major
scale, written in many positions.
