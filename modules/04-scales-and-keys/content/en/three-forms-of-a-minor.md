# Three forms of A minor

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. VIII secs. 85–88, pp. 33–34. Original prose.

The minor scale comes in three forms. The **primitive** form is just
what the signature gives, and it has no leading tone: its 7th sits a
whole step below the tonic. The **harmonic** form raises the 7th — in
A minor, G♯ — and pays for it with an awkward step and a half between
6 and 7. The **melodic** form raises the 6th as well on the way up, and
returns to the primitive form on the way down. Through all of this the
lower tetrachord never changes: only the upper half of the scale bends.
