# The leaning notes

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. VI pp. 24–26. Original prose; the figures are the book's own
(`assets/book/fig48-52.musicxml`).

The **appoggiatura** — the "leaning note" — is written small but lives
large: it takes the accent and real time from the note it graces, by
three tidy rules. Before a plain note it takes **half** the value;
before a dotted note, **two-thirds**; before a tied pair, the whole of
the **first** note.

Its opposite is the **acciaccatura**, the small note with the slashed
stem: "crushed" against its neighbor with no measurable time of its own,
and — the reliable distinction — **never accented**, where the
appoggiatura always is.

## Narration (video: TheLeaningNotes)

1. A small note that behaves like a big one: the appoggiatura leans on
   the beat and takes real time from its principal.
2. How much time is rule-work: half of a plain note, two-thirds of a
   dotted one, all of a tied note's first value.
3. The slashed small note is the acciaccatura — crushed in, instantly
   gone, never accented. One glance at the stem tells you which world
   you are in.
