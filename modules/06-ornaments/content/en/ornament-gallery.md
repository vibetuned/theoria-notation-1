# The ornament gallery

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. VI pp. 22–24. Original prose; the figures are the book's own
(`assets/book/fig40-47.musicxml`).

Ornaments are shorthand: one small sign standing for several notes. The
**trill** alternates rapidly with the *diatonic* neighbor above (modern
practice starts on the principal note). The **mordent** dips once to the
lower neighbor and returns; the **inverted mordent** touches the upper.
The **turn** circles its note — upper neighbor, note, lower neighbor,
note — timed by whether the sign sits over the note or after it, and
bent by any accidental written above or below the sign.

Gehrkens closes with a historian's shrug: ornaments are waning. But the
music of the eighteenth century cannot be read without them — which is
exactly why each sign here is shown, then sounded.

## Narration (video: TheOrnamentGallery)

1. One small sign, many notes. The trill: rapid alternation with the
   neighbor above — hear the sign unfold.
2. The mordent bites downward once and returns; its inverted twin
   touches the note above.
3. The turn draws a circle around its note: above, on, below, on.
4. Ornaments are going out of fashion, the book admits — but two
   centuries of music speak this shorthand fluently.
