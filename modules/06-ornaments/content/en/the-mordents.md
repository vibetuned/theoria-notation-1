# Mordents: one quick flick down or up

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. VI §§66–68, pp. 22–26. Original prose.

A **mordent** is a single, very fast flick: the written note, its
neighbour, and the written note again, all taken out of the note's own
time. With a short vertical line through the sign it dips to the note
*below* — Gehrkens calls this simply the mordent; today it is usually
called the **lower mordent**. Without the line it flicks to the note
*above*: the book's *inverted mordent*, today's **upper mordent** (also
called a short trill). Gehrkens himself wished the two were simply named
the downward and the upward mordent.
