# The trill: a rapid shake

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. VI §§64–65, pp. 22–26. Original prose.

Ornaments are shorthand: one small sign stands for several quick notes.
The **trill** (*tr*, often followed by a wavy line) is a rapid
alternation between the written note and the next note above it in the
scale — a half step or a whole step, whatever the key gives — lasting
the whole value of the note. Gehrkens reports that most writers of his
day begin the trill on the written note, and that is how it is shown
first here. In music from before about 1800 (Bach, Handel, Mozart),
players usually begin on the upper note instead. A trill that closes
with the note below and back, as here, is what the book calls a
*perfect* trill.
