# Same key, two names

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. III §27, p. 10. Original prose.

Every black key on the piano has two names: the key between F and G is
both F♯ and G♭. Even white keys have spare names: E♯ is played on the
F key, and C♭ on the B key. Two notations for one and the same pitch
are called **enharmonic**. The spelling a composer chooses depends on
the letters the music around it needs, which later lessons on scales
and chords will show.

## On screen (video: EnharmonicSpelling)

- title: Same key, two names
- pair: F sharp and G flat: two spellings, one piano key.
- white: White keys have spare names too. E sharp is F, and C flat is B.
- term: One sound written two ways: we call the two spellings enharmonic.
- name-f-sharp: F♯
- name-g-flat: G♭
- name-e-sharp: E♯
- name-f: F
- name-c-flat: C♭
- name-b: B
- remember: Enharmonic notes: one key, one sound, two spellings.
