# Double sharps and double flats

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. II §20 p. 7, ch. III §26 p. 10 (rules quoted from C. I. Rice,
*The Worcester Musical Manual*). Original prose.

A **double sharp** makes a note stand for the pitch two half steps (a
whole step) higher than its plain letter; a **double flat**, two half
steps lower. So F double sharp is played on the G key, and B double flat
on the A key. Why not just write G or A? Because sometimes the letter
must stay the same. Gehrkens passes on six rules from Charles I. Rice:
to raise a plain note a half step, sharp it; to raise a sharped note,
double-sharp it; to raise a flatted note, natural it. Lowering mirrors
them: flat a plain note, double-flat a flatted one, natural a sharped
one.
