# Sharp, flat and natural

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. II §§18–19 pp. 6–7, ch. III §21 p. 8. Original prose.

On the piano, a **half step** is the distance from one key to the very
next key, black or white. A **sharp** in front of a note makes it stand
for the pitch a half step higher; a **flat** makes it a half step lower;
a **natural** cancels either one. Gehrkens insists that a sharp does not
"raise" the note at all: the note stays on its line or space, and that
degree of the staff now stands for a different tone. F♯ is a tone in its
own right, just as G is.

- title: Sharp, flat and natural
- half: A half step: from one piano key to the very next one.
- sharp: A sharp makes the note a half step higher. F becomes F sharp.
- same-place: The note stays on the same space. Only the pitch it stands for changes.
- flat: A flat makes the note a half step lower. B becomes B flat.
- natural: A natural cancels the sharp or the flat. B flat goes back to B.
- name-f: F
- name-f-sharp: F♯
- name-b: B
- name-b-flat: B♭
- remember: Sharp: a half step higher. Flat: a half step lower. Natural: cancels them.
