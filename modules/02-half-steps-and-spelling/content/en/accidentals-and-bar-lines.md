# How long does an accidental last?

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. III §§24–25, p. 9 (Figs. 14–17). Original prose.

A sharp, flat or natural written in the course of the music is called
an **accidental**. It holds to the end of its measure: a later note on
the same line or space keeps it without a new sign. The bar line ends
it. The one exception is a **tie** across the bar line: the tied note
keeps the accidental until it stops sounding, and after that the plain
letter returns. Editors often print a reminder sign where a reader
might hesitate; Gehrkens notes that an accidental may simply "make
clear" a notation, changing nothing.

## On screen (video: AccidentalsAndBarLines)

- title: How long does an accidental last?
- measure: A sharp lasts to the end of its measure. This C is still C sharp.
- barline: The bar line cancels it. In the next measure, C is plain C again.
- courtesy: Many editions add a reminder natural. It changes nothing; it removes doubt.
- tie: A tie carries the sharp over the bar line, to the end of the tied note.
- after-tie: After the tie, C is plain C again.
- remember: An accidental lasts until the bar line. Only a tie carries it further.
