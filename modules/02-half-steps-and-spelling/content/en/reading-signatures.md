# Reading key signatures

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. III pp. 10–12. Original prose.

The **key signature** sets its accidentals once, at the clef, for the
whole piece — a standing order instead of a note-by-note request. But
every signature serves **two keys**: a major and its relative minor. The
book's test tells them apart without playing a note of the scale: look
at the **final bass chord** — built on DO, the piece is major; built on
LA, it is the relative minor.

Why the sharps arrive in their fixed order — F, C, G, D, A, E, B — and
the flats in the mirror of it, is the scales' own story, told in module
four. For now it is enough to read the signature as what it is: the
key's fingerprint.
