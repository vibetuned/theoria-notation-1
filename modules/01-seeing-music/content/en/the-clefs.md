# Clefs: naming the lines

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. I §§6–7 p. 3, ch. II §§15–17 pp. 5–6. Original prose.

On its own the staff only says "higher" and "lower". The **clef** tells
which pitch each degree stands for. The **G clef** began as a letter G:
its curl wraps around the second line, and that line is G. The **F
clef** began as a letter F: its two dots sit on either side of the
fourth line, and that line is F. From the named line, the letters run
up and down the alphabet A to G, one degree each. Change the clef and
the same line means a different note. A third clef, the **C clef**,
marks middle C on the line it centres on; violas still read it.
