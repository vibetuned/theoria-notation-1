# How notes are drawn

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. I pp. 1–4. Original prose.

A note is a **head**, usually a **stem**, sometimes a **hook** — and
each part has manners. Below the middle line the stem points **up from
the right side** of the head; above the middle line, **down from the
left**; on the line itself, either way serves symmetry. Hooks always
curl to the right. Eighth notes and shorter may trade their hooks for a
shared **beam**, its direction following the majority of the heads —
though vocal music keeps hooks wherever each note carries its own
syllable.

Small placements matter too: the dot after a note always sits **in a
space**, never on a line; whole and half rests hang and sit around the
middle of the staff. These rules are why pages of music look
inevitable — every mark has exactly one correct home.

1. A note is a head, sometimes a stem, sometimes a hook — and each part
   knows its manners.
2. Below the middle line the stem points up from the right side; above,
   down from the left. On the line itself, either serves.
3. Short notes may share a beam instead of hooks; and the dot after a
   note always sits in a space. Small rules — but they are why pages of
