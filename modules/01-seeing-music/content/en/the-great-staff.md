# The great staff and middle C

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. II §§12–13, p. 5. Original prose.

Piano music needs more room than one staff gives, so it joins a treble
staff and a bass staff into the **great staff** (today usually called
the grand staff). Between the two sits one short extra line: the home
of **middle C**, the C nearest the middle of the piano. Middle C belongs
to both staves. Short lines like it, called **ledger lines** (Gehrkens
spells them *leger*), can extend any staff upward or downward. Read
across the gap one degree at a time and the two staves join into one
continuous ladder of pitch.
