# Stems up, stems down

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. I §§1–4, pp. 1–2. Original prose.

On a staff with one melody, the **middle line** decides which way a stem
points. Below it, the stem goes up from the right side of the head;
above it, the stem goes down from the left side. On the middle line
itself either direction is allowed, and down is the usual modern
choice. Flags always curl to the right. Eighth notes and shorter can be
joined by a **beam** instead of flags; all stems in a beamed group then
point the same way, the way most of their heads would want.

## On screen (video: StemDirection)

- title: Stems up, stems down
- below: Below the middle line, the stem goes up, on the right side of the head.
- above: Above the middle line, the stem goes down, on the left side.
- middle: On the middle line, either way is fine. Down is the usual choice.
- flags: Flags always curl to the right, whichever way the stem points.
- beams: Beamed notes share one direction: the one most of their heads would take.
- remember: Low notes: stem up, on the right. High notes: stem down, on the left.
