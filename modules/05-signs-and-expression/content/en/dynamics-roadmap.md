# Dynamics and the roadmap

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. IV pp. 13–16 and ch. XIII pp. 56–61. Original prose; the
repeat figure is the book's own (`assets/book/fig25-27.musicxml`).

**Dynamics** are relative, never absolute: the ladder *ppp* through
*fff* orders loudness, and three swells shape it — *sforzando* on one
tone, *rinforzando* lifting a passage at once, *crescendo* growing by
degrees. Then the **roadmap**: repeat dots, first and second endings,
*D.C.*, *D.S.* with its segno, *fine*, the fermata — the signs that
steer a performance through the page's shortcuts. And when the staff
runs out of room, the octave signs fold its range outward: *8va* lifts a
passage an octave, *8va bassa* drops it, *loco* brings it home.

## Narration (video: LoudSoftAndTheRoadmap)

1. Loudness in music is relative: the ladder from ppp to fff orders it;
   the piece decides what forte means tonight.
2. Three ways to swell: strike one tone (sforzando), lift a passage at
   once (rinforzando), or grow by degrees (crescendo).
3. The roadmap signs steer the journey: repeats, endings, da capo, dal
   segno, fine.
4. And when the staff runs out of room, 8va folds the range up, 8va
   bassa down, and loco brings it home.
