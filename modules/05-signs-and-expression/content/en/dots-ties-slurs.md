# Dots, ties, and slurs

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. V pp. 17–21. Original prose; the figures are the book's own
(`assets/book/fig30-33.musicxml`).

The connective tissue of notation. The **tie** welds two notes of one
pitch into a single sound, even across a barline — the only way a note
outlives its measure. The **slur** looks similar and means four
different things by context: play *legato*; mark a phrase; carry one
syllable through many notes; or bracket a tuplet. **Dot arithmetic** is
exact — one dot makes 3/2 of the value, two 7/4, three 15/8 — and the
articulation marks (staccato dot, tenuto dash, accent wedge) govern how
a tone is *left* as much as how it is struck.

## Narration (video: DotsTiesSlurs)

1. The tie welds two notes of one pitch into one sound — the only way a
   note outlives its barline.
2. The slur is a chameleon: legato, phrase-mark, one syllable across
   many notes, or a tuplet's bracket. Context decides.
3. Dots are arithmetic: three halves, seven quarters, fifteen eighths of
   the plain value.
4. And the little marks — dot, dash, wedge — decide how a note leaves the
   room.
