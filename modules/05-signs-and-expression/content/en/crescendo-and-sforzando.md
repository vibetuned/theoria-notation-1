# Hairpins and sf: louder and softer

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. XIII §§122–126, pp. 56–61. Original prose.

Loudness can change on the way. An opening **hairpin** — two lines
spreading apart — is a **crescendo**: grow louder little by little. A
closing hairpin is a **diminuendo** (or *decrescendo*): grow softer.
The words *cresc.* and *dim.* ask for the same thing. Gehrkens sets a
third sign beside them: **sf** (*sforzando*) puts a sudden strong accent
on one single note, after which the music is as soft as before. On the
piano a crescendo is made from note to note, each one struck a little
harder than the last.
