# Staccato, tenuto, accent, marcato

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. V §§52–59, pp. 17–21. Original prose.

Small marks over or under a note head tell you *how* to play the note.
A **dot** (staccato) means short: sound the note and let go at once. A
short **line** (tenuto) means the opposite: hold it for its full value,
leaning on it a little. The **accent** `>` asks for a note louder than
its neighbours. Gehrkens lists `>` and `^` side by side as accent marks;
modern usage reads the `^` (**marcato**) as the stronger, heavier one.
He also stresses (ch. XIII §122) that accents are relative: an accent in
a soft passage is gentler than one in a loud passage.
