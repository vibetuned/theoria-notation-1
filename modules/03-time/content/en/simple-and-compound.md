# Simple and compound meter

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. X §§99–101, pp. 45–46. Original prose.

Count the beats in a measure and you get two, three or four. Then look
inside one beat. In **simple** meter each beat splits into two: 2/4,
3/4 and 4/4. In **compound** meter each beat is a dotted note that
splits into three: 6/8, 9/8 and 12/8 have two, three and four beats.
This is the usage that has won out today. Gehrkens reports it as one
English writer's view; he himself called any measure built from smaller
ones "compound" (four beats as two plus two). Both agree on the ear's
verdict: 6/8 at a lively tempo is felt in two beats, a dotted quarter
each.
