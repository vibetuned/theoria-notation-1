# The tempo words

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), chs. XI–XII pp. 48–55. Original prose.

Tempo runs on the eight-family Italian ladder — *grave*, *largo*,
*larghetto*, *andante*, *moderato*, *allegro*, *vivace*, *presto* — and
is pinned exactly by the **metronome**'s clicks per minute (M.M., for
Maelzel's machine). The words bend in two distinct ways: **gradually**,
with *ritardando* and *accelerando* (and their cousins *rallentando* and
*stringendo*), or **at once**, with *meno mosso* and *più mosso*; *a
tempo* and *tempo primo* restore what was bent. *Rubato* borrows time
and pays it back inside the phrase.

Speak precisely and the three ideas stay apart: three-quarter
**measure**, andante **tempo**, dotted **rhythm** — the word "time" can
retire.

## Narration (video: TheTempoWords)

1. Eight families of speed, from grave to presto — a ladder every score
   assumes you know.
2. The metronome pins the rungs precisely; the words bend them —
   gradually with ritardando and accelerando, instantly with meno mosso
   and più mosso.
3. Speak precisely and the three ideas stay apart: three-quarter
   measure, andante tempo, dotted rhythm. The word "time" can retire.
