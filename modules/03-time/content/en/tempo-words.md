# Tempo words and the metronome

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. XI §§107–109, pp. 48–51. Original prose.

Tempo marks are written in Italian nearly everywhere, which Gehrkens
defends as a kind of musical Esperanto: a player anywhere can read
them. He groups the words from slowest to fastest: grave, largo,
larghetto, andante, moderato, allegro, vivace, presto. The words also
carry a mood (andante means "walking", allegro "cheerful"), and no two
composers mean exactly the same speed by them. For an exact speed there
is the **metronome** mark: M.M. 92 (Maelzel's Metronome) means 92 beats
a minute.
