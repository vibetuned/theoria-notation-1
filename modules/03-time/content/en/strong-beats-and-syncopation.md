# Strong beats and syncopation

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. X §§97–98 and §100, pp. 44–46 (after Fig. 56). Original prose.

The beats of a measure are not equal. Beat 1 carries the strongest
accent; in a four-beat measure, beat 3 carries a lighter one, and beats
2 and 4 are weak. When the music puts its accent on a beat that is
normally weak, for example by starting a long note on beat 2, the
regular pattern is briefly upset. That is **syncopation**, and
Gehrkens's own example does exactly this: a first measure accented on 1
and 3, and a later one whose only accent falls on 2.
