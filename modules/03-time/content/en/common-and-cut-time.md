# Common time and cut time

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), ch. X §103, p. 47. Original prose.

Two time signatures are written as signs instead of numbers. **Common
time**, a C, means 4/4: four quarter-note beats in a measure. **Cut
time** (alla breve), a C with a vertical line through it, means 2/2:
the half note gets the beat, so the same notes are counted in two and
usually move along faster. Gehrkens points out that the C is not the
letter C at all but a broken circle: in medieval notation a full circle
marked three-beat ("perfect") measure, and the broken circle two-beat
("imperfect") measure.

- title: Common time and cut time
- common: This sign means 4/4: four quarter-note beats. It is called common time.
- count-four: Counted in four.
- cut: With a line through it, it means 2/2: two half-note beats. This is cut time.
- count-two: The same notes, counted in two. They feel lighter and quicker.
- circle: The sign is not the letter C. It is a broken circle.
- old: In medieval notation a full circle meant three beats; a broken circle, two.
- label-three: three beats
- label-two: two beats
- remember: C means 4/4, common time. C with a line means 2/2, cut time.
