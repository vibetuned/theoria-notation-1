# How music is built

**Source**: Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes,
1914), chs. XIV–XVI pp. 62–81. Original prose; the figures are the
book's own (`assets/book/fig57-60.musicxml`, `beethoven.musicxml`).

Gehrkens' sharpest distinction: **form** is a *plan* (the fugue, the
sonata), **style** a *manner of building* (counterpoint, like Gothic
architecture). Two styles cover music — **monophonic**, one melody
carried by support, and **polyphonic**, every voice a melody. In the
polyphonic family, imitation breeds the species: the **canon** imitates
strictly at any interval, the **round** is a canon at the unison, and
the **fugue** imitates at the dominant, its answer waiting for the
subject to finish.

Size builds by nesting: a **phrase** (about four measures) plus its
answering phrase makes a **period** — incomplete cadence asking,
complete cadence answering. Periods stack into the two- and three-part
**song forms**; themes develop by transposition, augmentation,
inversion; the **rondo** keeps returning home between contrasts; and
**sonata-form** crowns the system with its three acts — exposition,
development, recapitulation. The vocal world (ch. XVI) gets its own
genre map, from anthem and chorale to cantata, oratorio, and opera.

## Narration (video: FormAndStyle)

1. A fugue is a form — a plan. Counterpoint is a style — a manner of
   building. Keep the words apart and the whole map opens.
2. Listen to a canon: one melody, strictly imitated. A round is the same
   trick at the unison.
3. Two styles cover everything: one melody carried, or every voice a
   melody.
4. And the fugue's rule of courtesy: the answer enters at the dominant,
   only after the subject has said its piece.

## Narration (video: PhraseToSonata)

1. Music builds by nesting. A phrase is about four measures; question
   and answer together make a period.
2. Periods stack into song forms — Beethoven's Ninth carries the most
   famous theme in the world exactly this way.
3. At the summit, sonata-form: exposition states, development argues,
   recapitulation reconciles.
4. Forms are maps. Learn a handful, and wherever the music goes, you
   know where you are.
