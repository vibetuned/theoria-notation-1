# Gehrkens digests (working notes, 2026-07-10)

Gehrkens, Music Notation and Terminology (A. S. Barnes, 1914; PD).
Twenty chapters + appendices; extraction is a Gutenberg-style web edition
(no scan-page markers — printed pages inferred from the TOC anchors).
`assets/book/` = the extraction's mxl-midi (62 files, MIDI-derived:
pitches/durations reliable, notation details unverified — check the page
image before quoting one in a scene). failures.txt empty; figures without
files (fig06-11, 24, 29) never had audio in the source.

## Chapters (teachable cores)

1. **Correct notation (1-4)** — note anatomy (head/stem/hook), stem
   direction (below 3rd line up, above down), beaming, rest placement,
   dot on a space. Files fig01-05.
2. **Symbols (5-7)** — staff, GREAT STAFF (treble+bass+middle-C leger),
   degrees, clefs as pitch-assigners, movable C; sharps/flats make a
   DEGREE represent a different pitch (Gehrkens is emphatic). No files.
3. **Symbols cont. (8-12)** — natural; key signature; major-vs-minor test
   (final bass chord DO vs LA); accidentals expire at the barline; Rice's
   six alteration rules (figs 18-23); enharmonics; note/rest table.
4. **Abbreviations (13-16)** — repeats, D.C./D.S./fine/coda, fermata,
   8va/loco, octave-register names (great G, one-lined D...).
5. **Abbreviations cont. (17-21)** — dot arithmetic (3/2, 7/4, 15/8),
   tie, the slur's four uses, staccato/tenuto/accents, arpeggio line.
6. **Embellishments (22-26)** — trill, mordent/inverted, turn, appoggiatura
   value rules (half; two-thirds of dotted; tied = first note),
   acciaccatura. Files fig40-52 complete — best audio coverage.
7. **Scales (27-32)** — scale vs key; diatonic/chromatic/whole-tone; the
   major rule (half steps 3-4, 7-8); TETRACHORDS (upper of one sharp key =
   lower of the next, figs 54-55); 15 positions; files: scales.musicxml
   (all 15), jasmine.musicxml (pentatonic).
8. **Scales cont. (33-41)** — minor = smaller third; natural/harmonic
   (aug 2nd 6-7)/melodic patterns; relative vs tonic minor; degree names
   with etymologies; solfège (minor is LA-based); chromatic spelling;
   whole-tone scale. Files: minor.musicxml (3 forms), chromatic.musicxml,
   stjohn.musicxml (solfège origin hymn). Minor-scales chart image-only.
9. **Auxiliary words (42-43)** — the Italian particles (assai, con, meno,
   molto, non, più, poco, sempre, senza...). One video beat.
10. **Measure (44-47)** — measure for the EAR; rhythm vs measure;
    syncopation (fig56); simple vs compound; accent placements; C is a
    broken circle, not a letter.
11. **Tempo (48-51)** — "time" abuse → rhythm/measure/tempo; metronome;
    the eight-group ladder grave→prestissimo.
12. **Tempo cont. (52-55)** — gradual (rit./accel.) vs immediate (più
    mosso/meno mosso) changes; a tempo; rubato; l'istesso tempo.
13. **Dynamics (56-61)** — relative loudness; ppp→fff ladder; sforzando
    vs rinforzando vs crescendo; two crescendo kinds; pedal signs.
14. **Forms and styles (62-66)** — form vs STYLE (fugue is a form,
    counterpoint a style); monophonic vs polyphonic; canon (fig57 Mozart),
    round, fugue (subject/answer).
15. **Forms cont. (67-75)** — phrase (4 mm.) → period (8, antecedent +
    consequent, figs 58-59) → two-/three-part song forms (beethoven.musicxml
    = Ninth theme); thematic development (fig60); rondo; suite; sonata
    plan; SONATA-FORM (exposition/development/recap); program music.
16. **Vocal music (76-81)** — anthem/motet/madrigal/chorale/mass/cantata/
    oratorio/opera; recitative (fig61 Messiah) vs aria vs lied vs ballad.
17. **Rhythm/melody/harmony/intervals (82-86)** — four elements; harmonic
    vs melodic intervals (figs 62-63); number name by staff degrees +
    quality by half steps (full table secs. 185-192); fig64 = intervals
    above the tonic all major/perfect.
18. **Chords, cadences (87-94)** — triad qualities (M/m/dim/aug); I, IV, V;
    inversions + figured bass; CADENCES with author definitions:
    authentic V-I (perfect = root on top, else imperfect), plagal IV-I
    ("amen"), half (phrase ends on V preceded by I), deceptive (V to
    other-than-I, commonest V-vi); sequence; suspension/retardation/
    anticipation; pedal point. Figures 65-79 ALL have files +
    triads.musicxml. Strongest checkable chapter.
19-20. **Glossaries (95-100)** — voice ranges, consonance/dissonance,
    genres, opus/score/tutti. Mine for definitions.

## Module plan (9; exercises ONLY in 4, 8, 9)

1. 01-seeing-music (ch. 2, 1, 3a) — staff/great staff; clefs and names;
   note shapes and how they're drawn.
2. 02-half-steps-and-spelling (ch. 2 §18-20, 3) — degree modifiers;
   barline rule + Rice's rules; signatures and the major/minor test.
3. 03-time (ch. 10-12) — beat, rhythm vs measure, syncopation; simple vs
   compound, C/cut-C; tempo ladder.
4. 04-scales-and-keys (ch. 7, 8) — EXERCISES (scale-steps) — major rule +
   tetrachords; 15 positions + relative minor; three minor forms +
   solfège; chromatic/whole-tone tour.
5. 05-signs-and-expression (ch. 5, 13, 4) — dots/ties/slurs/articulation;
   dynamics; navigation signs.
6. 06-ornaments (ch. 6) — sign gallery with audio; appoggiatura rules.
7. 07-intervals (ch. 17) — number names; qualities table; inversion.
8. 08-chords-and-cadences (ch. 18) — EXERCISES (cadence recognition) —
   triads/inversions; the four cadences (figs 65-79 all playable).
9. 09-form (ch. 14-16) — EXERCISES (form classification) — form vs style;
   phrase→period→song forms; sonata-form; vocal genres.
